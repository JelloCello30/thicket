Get Thicket for Chrome

Install it, and within seconds your open tabs become a short list of the things you're actually doing. No setup, no account required.

The Chrome Web Store listing is on its way.

Until it's live, you can run Thicket from source in about a minute — everything works, including updates when you pull:

  1. Clone github.com/JelloCello30/thicket and run pnpm install && pnpm build
  2. Open chrome://extensions, turn on Developer mode
  3. Click “Load unpacked” and pick apps/extension/dist

Chrome 121 or newer. Works on Edge, Brave, and Arc too.

What happens next

  1. 1

    Thicket looks at your open tabs — titles and addresses, right on your device.

  2. 2

    A few seconds later: your tabs, grouped into the projects behind them.

  3. 3

    Close a group when you're done with it. Everything stays recoverable.

The permissions, explained

Chrome will show you what Thicket can access. Here's the honest version of why:

Read your browsing history (tabs)
This is Chrome's blanket wording for seeing tab titles and addresses — the raw material for grouping. Thicket reads your open tabs; it does not read your browser history file.
Tab groups
So your Thicket groups can appear as native colored groups in the tab strip. You can turn the mirroring off.
Storage
Your groups, workspaces, and page memory live in the extension's local storage on your machine.
Alarms
Two scheduled jobs: a housekeeping flush, and a daily pass that deletes page memory older than 7 days. A background extension cannot hold a timer while it sleeps.
Site icons
Draws each site's icon next to its tab, using the copy Chrome already has. No favicon service is contacted, so no list of the sites you visit is sent anywhere.
Not requested: access to websites
Thicket asks for no access to any site, optional or otherwise. It cannot read the contents of a page, and Chrome will not show it on the permission list.
